Topic 2

🌱 Plants

Let's explore the parts of a plant, what plants need to stay alive and grow, and how bees help flowers make seeds!

🌼 2.1 Parts of Plants

🌸 Flower

Helps the plant to reproduce and make new seeds.

🍃 Leaves

Use sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide to make food for the plant.

🌿 Stem

Holds the plant up towards the light and transports water from roots to leaves.

🪴 Roots

Anchor the plant in the soil and absorb water & minerals.

☀️ 2.2 – 2.4 What Plants Need to Grow

💧

Water (2.2)

Roots absorb water from the soil. Water travels up the stem to the leaves. Without water, plants wilt and die.

☀️

Sunlight (2.3)

Plants make their own food using sunlight! A plant kept in the dark grows tall, weak, and has yellow/pale leaves.

🌡️

Warmth (2.4)

Seeds need warmth to germinate (start growing). Seeds placed in a cold fridge shrivel up and do not germinate.

🐝 2.5 – 2.6 Healthy Plants & Honeybees

🌾 Fertilisers & Irrigation

  • Fertilisers add extra minerals to the soil so plants grow bigger, stronger, and healthier.
  • Irrigation means watering dry land (like deserts) on purpose using pipes or sprinklers so crops can grow.

🐝 Why Bees Are Important

  • Bees are attracted by brightly coloured petals and sweet smell.
  • They drink nectar and transfer pollen from flower to flower so plants can make seeds.
  • Pesticides, habitat loss, and climate change are harming bees.
Topic 3.1 – 3.3

🦋 Animals & Life Cycles

Learn about 2D & 3D models, the 6 main animal groups, and how animals grow and change!

🧊 3.1 Models & Representations

📄 Two-Dimensional (2D) Models

Flat drawings, pictures, or diagrams on paper (for example, a diagram of the heart).

🧩 Three-Dimensional (3D) Models

Physical models you can touch, turn around, and sometimes take apart to see the inside (like a clay brain model).

🦁 3.2 Classifying Animals (6 Main Groups)

🐻

Mammals

Have fur or hair, are warm-blooded, give birth to live young, and feed babies with milk. (Fun fact: A platypus lays eggs, but is still a mammal!)

🦎

Reptiles

Have dry scales, are cold-blooded, and lay eggs. Examples: snake, lizard, turtle, crocodile.

🐞

Insects

Have 6 legs, antennae, and a hard outer shell. (Watch out: Spiders have 8 legs, so they are NOT insects!)

🐟

Fish

Live in water, have fins instead of legs, are covered in scales, and breathe using gills.

🦅

Birds

Have feathers, a beak, 2 legs, and 2 wings. They are warm-blooded and lay eggs.

🐸

Amphibians

Live in water and on land. Breathe with gills when young, then lungs & moist skin as adults.

🔄 3.3 Growing & Changing (Life Cycles)

🦋 Butterfly Life Cycle (Metamorphosis)

🐸 Frog Life Cycle

Frogs also change their appearance completely as they grow:

  1. Eggs: Female frog lays eggs in water.
  2. Tadpole: Hatches from egg and breathes with gills.
  3. Tadpole with legs: Grows back legs first, then front legs, and starts breathing with lungs.
  4. Froglet: Tail shrinks and it can leave the water.
  5. Adult Frog: Lives on land and in water.
Topic 3.4 – 3.7

🧠 Your Amazing Human Body

Discover how your brain, heart, stomach & intestines, and lungs work together to keep you alive!

🧠 3.4 Your Brain

🛡️ Your brain is very fragile! It is protected by your skull and a layer of fluid.

1. Cerebrum: Largest, folded part. Controls thinking, memory, senses, and emotions.
2. Cerebellum: Helps with movement and balance.
3. Brain Stem: Controls automatic actions: heart rate, breathing, sleeping, and eating.

❤️ 3.5 Your Heart

  • The heart is a strong muscle pump that never gets tired.
  • It pumps blood to the lungs to pick up oxygen, then pumps that oxygen-rich blood around the whole body.
  • When we exercise, our muscles need more oxygen, so our heart beats faster and our pulse rate increases.

🫁 3.7 Your Lungs

  • Protected by your ribcage.
  • When you breathe in (inhale), your lungs expand to take in oxygen.
  • When you breathe out (exhale), your lungs contract to push out carbon dioxide.

🍎 3.6 Your Stomach & Intestines (Digestion)

  1. Mouth: Teeth chew food into small pieces.
  2. Oesophagus: Tube that carries food from mouth to stomach.
  3. Stomach: Breaks down food into even smaller pieces.
  4. Small Intestine: Breaks down food further so nutrients can pass into the blood.
  5. Large Intestine: Unwanted food leaves the body as waste.
